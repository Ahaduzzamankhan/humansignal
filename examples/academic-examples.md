# Academic Examples

Version 2.1.0 · Human Signal

Ten worked examples in academic prose. Each follows the seven-part structure used throughout the example library.

**Note on citations:** no example invents a reference. Where a citation is needed, the revised text uses a bracketed placeholder such as `[source]` and the assessment states that the writer must supply a verified reference. Fabricating a citation to make an example look complete would violate the integrity rules in [../references/academic-writing.md](../references/academic-writing.md).

**Note on figures:** all samples, effect sizes, and study counts in these examples are illustrative. Replace them with the writer's verified data before use.

## ACA-01 — The importance opening

**Original:** "In recent years, artificial intelligence has become increasingly important in education, and researchers around the world have paid growing attention to its potential."

**Issues:** Importance assertion; unnamed body of researchers; "potential" without content; no research question.

**Analysis:** The opening contains three trends and no specifics: a time frame, a field, and a group. None is dated, quantified, or cited. The paragraph could introduce any paper on the topic.

**Explanation:** The reader learns that the topic is popular, not what this paper asks or why the question is unresolved. The general claim also implies a literature consensus without evidence.

**Revised:** "Automated essay scoring is now used in state testing programs, yet the score agreement between systems and human raters varies widely across student populations [source]. This study asks whether that variation tracks differences in writing prompt genre."

**Improvements:** A dated, attributable practice replaces "increasingly important"; a specific gap replaces "growing attention"; the research question is explicit.

**Meaning preserved:** Yes, at a much narrower scope. The writer must supply a source for the adoption claim and confirm that the variation is the gap being studied.

## ACA-02 — Literature list instead of synthesis

**Original:** "Smith studied motivation in online courses. Jones examined engagement in MOOCs. Brown looked at retention. All of these researchers found that motivation matters."

**Issues:** Author-by-author summary; three studies reduced to topics; a consensus claim with no comparison.

**Analysis:** Each sentence names a researcher and a topic, but none states a finding, a method, or a disagreement. The final sentence asserts agreement without evidence.

**Explanation:** A literature review synthesizes; it does not inventory. The reader needs to know what the studies found, how they measured it, and where they diverge.

**Revised:** "Studies of online course completion disagree about whether motivation or course design explains more variance. Two survey studies attribute most dropout to self-reported motivation, while a platform-log study finds that deadline structure predicts completion more strongly than survey scores [sources]. The disagreement may reflect measurement: surveys capture intention, logs capture behavior."

**Improvements:** The paragraph organizes by question, compares findings, and offers a reason for the disagreement.

**Meaning preserved:** No, unless the writer's sources actually reported these findings. The revision is a structural model; the specific claims must come from the cited studies.

## ACA-03 — Causal overreach

**Original:** "Students who used the tutoring system improved their grades, which proves that the system causes better academic performance."

**Issues:** "Proves"; causal claim from an observational comparison; no control for self-selection.

**Analysis:** The sentence reports an association between use and grades and then converts it into a causal conclusion. Students who choose tutoring may differ from those who do not in motivation, prior achievement, or course load.

**Explanation:** The design cannot separate the system's effect from the characteristics of the students who used it. "Proves" is too strong even for a randomized trial; it is certainly too strong here.

**Revised:** "Students who used the tutoring system earned higher final grades than those who did not (mean difference 0.4 points). Because students chose whether to use the system, the difference may reflect prior achievement or motivation rather than the tutoring itself."

**Improvements:** The association is reported with its magnitude, and the causal language is replaced by a plausible alternative explanation.

**Meaning preserved:** Yes. The claim is weakened to match the design, which is a correction rather than a distortion.

## ACA-04 — Unsupported consensus

**Original:** "It is well known that feedback improves learning, and most researchers agree that immediate feedback is superior to delayed feedback."

**Issues:** "Well known"; unnamed consensus; "superior" without a measure or population.

**Analysis:** Both clauses appeal to shared belief. Neither names a source, a meta-analysis, or a condition. "Immediate" and "delayed" are also undefined in duration.

**Explanation:** Consensus claims are empirical claims and require evidence. The literature on feedback timing is in fact conditional: immediate feedback can help procedural tasks and interfere with retention in some conceptual tasks.

**Revised:** "Feedback generally improves learning, though the effect depends on timing and task. Research on feedback timing finds that immediate feedback can help procedural performance while delayed feedback can produce better retention on transfer tasks [source]."

**Improvements:** A conditional finding replaces a consensus assertion, and the source is marked.

**Meaning preserved:** No. The original claimed a general superiority; the revision reports a conditional pattern. The writer must supply a source that supports it before using this revision.

## ACA-05 — Vague methods

**Original:** "Participants were asked to complete a survey about their experiences, and the data were analyzed to find patterns."

**Issues:** No sample size, recruitment method, instrument, scale, or analysis procedure.

**Analysis:** The sentence describes a study in terms that would fit thousands of studies. "Find patterns" does not name an analytic method.

**Explanation:** A methods section must let a reader judge validity and, where possible, reproduce the analysis. This description supports neither.

**Revised:** "We recruited 214 undergraduate students through the departmental participant pool. Participants completed the 18-item Engagement Scale (7-point Likert; α = .87) and reported weekly study hours. We used linear regression with engagement as the outcome and study hours, year, and first-generation status as predictors."

**Improvements:** Sample, recruitment, instrument, reliability, variables, and model are specified.

**Meaning preserved:** No, unless these were the actual procedures. The revision is a template for the required level of detail.

## ACA-06 — Interpretation in the results

**Original:** "The treatment group scored higher, which is surprising and suggests that the intervention works better than expected."

**Issues:** Interpretation inside Results; "surprising" without a prior expectation; "works better" undefined.

**Analysis:** The sentence combines an observation ("scored higher"), an emotional reaction ("surprising"), and a causal interpretation ("intervention works"). Results sections should report the first and leave the others to the Discussion.

**Explanation:** Mixing observation and interpretation makes it impossible for the reader to separate what was measured from what the author concludes.

**Revised:** "The treatment group's mean score was 6.2 points higher than the control group's (95% CI 2.1–10.3; d = 0.41)."

**Improvements:** The finding is reported with uncertainty and effect size, without interpretation.

**Meaning preserved:** Yes for the observation. The interpretation is removed from Results and should appear in the Discussion, where it can be argued.

## ACA-07 — Generic limitations

**Original:** "This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings."

**Issues:** Announcement without content; no limitation named; no link to conclusions.

**Analysis:** The sentence performs the gesture of reflexivity without doing the work. It does not tell the reader which findings are affected or how.

**Explanation:** Limitations matter when they bound a specific claim. A generic sentence gives the reader no way to adjust their confidence.

**Revised:** "The sample came from one university and overrepresented first-year students, so the estimates may not generalize to institutions with different demographics. The self-report measure also cannot distinguish actual study time from perceived study time, which may inflate the association with grades."

**Improvements:** Two limitations are named, and each is connected to a specific claim.

**Meaning preserved:** Yes, and it adds the content the original promised.

## ACA-08 — Conclusion inflation

**Original:** "These findings prove that the intervention is effective for all students and should be implemented nationwide."

**Issues:** "Prove"; universal scope; policy recommendation beyond the design.

**Analysis:** The conclusion moves from a sample to a national population and from an effect to an implementation mandate. Neither step is supported by the described design.

**Explanation:** Effectiveness in one sample does not establish effectiveness everywhere, and evidence of benefit does not by itself settle cost, feasibility, or equity.

**Revised:** "The intervention improved the primary outcome in this sample. A multi-site trial is needed before considering wider adoption, particularly to test whether the effect holds in schools with different class sizes and staffing levels."

**Improvements:** The claim is bounded, and the next step is specific.

**Meaning preserved:** Partly. The recommendation is retained as a proposal rather than a mandate; the scope is corrected.

## ACA-09 — Over-hedged claim

**Original:** "It may perhaps be tentatively suggested that there could potentially be a possible relationship between the variables."

**Issues:** Five hedges; no direction, magnitude, or measure; passive frame.

**Analysis:** The sentence contains a single proposition — the variables are related — wrapped in markers that do not identify the source of uncertainty. The reader cannot tell whether the relationship was observed, expected, or hypothesized.

**Explanation:** Hedging should locate uncertainty, not multiply it. One precise qualifier is more honest than five vague ones.

**Revised:** "The two variables were positively associated in the observed sample (r = .34), though the cross-sectional design cannot establish direction."

**Improvements:** The relationship, its strength, and the design limitation are stated.

**Meaning preserved:** Yes, if the analysis produced this correlation. If no estimate exists, the honest version is: "The data are consistent with a positive association, but the sample is too small to estimate it reliably."

## ACA-10 — Abstract that repeats the title

**Original:** "This paper is about teacher retention. Teacher retention is an important issue in education. We study the factors that affect teacher retention."

**Issues:** Title restatement; importance claim; "factors" unspecified; no finding.

**Analysis:** Three sentences consume the abstract's most valuable space without stating a result. The reader already knows the topic from the title.

**Explanation:** An abstract should let a reader decide whether to read the paper. That requires the question, the method, the main finding, and the implication.

**Revised:** "Teacher turnover in rural districts is often attributed to pay, but administrative support predicts departure more strongly in the panel data analyzed here. Using eight years of district records (n = 412), we find that a one-standard-deviation increase in reported administrative support is associated with a 23% lower departure rate, while salary differences show no consistent association. The results suggest that retention policy should target working conditions as well as compensation."

**Improvements:** Question, data, method, finding, magnitude, and implication are present.

**Meaning preserved:** No, unless these are the paper's actual results. The revision demonstrates the required structure and must be replaced with the writer's findings.

## Cross-example observations

- **Correction is not distortion.** ACA-03 and ACA-08 weaken claims because the original claims outran the design. That is a legitimate edit, and it should be disclosed.
- **Templates are not content.** ACA-05 and ACA-10 show the required shape; they do not supply results.
- **No invented sources.** Every citation placeholder marks a verification task for the writer.
- **Limitations belong next to the claims they limit.** Generic limitation paragraphs fail even when they are sincere.
